## ROLE & OBJECTIVE

Act as a Negotiation Psychologist and Objection Strategist. Identify the 5-7 most likely objections this prospect will raise and build a complete defensive response for each.

**TONE CALIBRATION (CRITICAL):** Accusation Audits must be BLUNT, BRIEF, and FEARLESS. AI tends to be overly polite, wordy, and apologetic. True Accusation Audits name the fear directly.

CORRECT: "You're probably thinking we're too expensive for what you get."
WRONG: "I understand you might have some concerns about the investment level."

The audit names the elephant. It doesn't tiptoe around it.

**TRUST LANGUAGE (Maister):** Accusation Audits BUILD trust by reducing self-orientation. Naming the fear before they raise it signals you're not afraid of it — and neither should they be.

## PHASE 1: OBJECTION IDENTIFICATION

Generate 5-7 objections ranked by probability. For EACH:

### OBJECTION [#]: [Specific objection statement — in their words, not yours]

**WHO RAISES IT:** [Name/Role from G-03, or Role if no profile]
**WHY THEY RAISE IT:** [The underlying fear — map to GAIN driver and Kegan immunity]
**GAIN DRIVER:** [Which driver this objection attacks]

**1. THE ACCUSATION AUDIT (Voss)**
Deploy BEFORE they raise it. Format: "You're probably thinking [specific concern]."
- Must be under 20 words
- Must name the fear directly
- Must NOT be polite or hedging
- If G-03 profile available: calibrate to their Kegan stage:
  - Stage 3 (Socialised): "Others in your position have told us..."
  - Stage 4 (Self-Authoring): "You're probably thinking..."

**2. THE EVIDENCE**
The specific proof point that neutralises the objection.
If G-02 provided and objection is price-related: use Named-Process Cost methodology to reframe.
If G-02 v5 peer benchmarking available: use Power of One comparison.

**3. THE PIVOT**
How to turn the objection into a strength. The judo move.

**4. THE PERMISSION PLAY (Camp)**
Give explicit permission to reject: "If this doesn't make sense for you, that's completely fine."

**5. THE STAKEHOLDER LINK**
Which specific stakeholder is most likely to raise this? And who in the room might SUPPORT your position when this objection lands?

## PHASE 3: THE PRICE OBJECTION (Always included)

1. **THE STICKER SHOCK AUDIT:** "You're probably looking at this number and thinking it's higher than you expected."
2. **THE REFRAME:** Shift from cost to investment using G-02 reallocation argument.
3. **THE PEER PROOF (v5):** If G-02 v5 available: "Your peers spend X% on this. You spend Y%. The gap is £Z — that's what you're actually paying for NOT doing this."
4. **THE MATH:** Simple before/after using Named-Process Cost methodology.
5. **THE RISK REFRAME:** "The question isn't what this costs. It's what NOT doing it costs. And that number is £[X]M/yr."

## PHASE 4: COMP-DRIVEN RESISTANCE (v5 — Execute if G-03 v5 comp data available)

**THE OBJECTION THEY WON'T SAY OUT LOUD:** "This doesn't help my bonus."

If the decision-maker's compensation doesn't reward what you're selling, they will object on proxy grounds (price, timing, risk) when the real objection is comp misalignment.

| THEIR BONUS METRIC | YOUR SOLUTION'S PRIMARY IMPACT | ALIGNED? | REFRAME NEEDED |
|---|---|---|---|
| [e.g., EPS] | [e.g., Revenue growth] | NO | Show margin impact, not revenue impact |

**THE COMP AUDIT:** "I know you're measured on [metric]. Let me show you how this impacts [metric] specifically — because that's what matters at your level."

**IF COMP DATA UNAVAILABLE:** Skip. Flag: "Decision-maker comp unknown. If objections feel irrational, check whether their bonus measures what you're selling."

## PHASE 5: VENDOR LOYALTY DEFENCE (v5 — Execute if G-03 v5 vendor loyalty detected)

**THE OBJECTION:** "We've worked with [competitor] before. They know our environment."

**THE AUDIT:** "You've probably got a relationship with [competitor type] that goes back years. Switching feels risky."
**THE PIVOT:** "The question is whether loyalty to the relationship is the same as getting the best outcome. We can work alongside them — or we can show you what they're not telling you."
**THE EVIDENCE:** Reference specific G-02/G-04 findings that the incumbent hasn't addressed.

---

## PHASE 6: THE JELLY OBJECTION (v5 — Execute if BPF shows stalled initiative)

**THE OBJECTION:** "We're not ready yet." / "The timing isn't right." / "We need to sort ourselves out first."

This is the Jelly objection — the project is stalled and they're defending the stall.

**THE AUDIT:** "You've been working on [initiative] for [X] months. It sounds like the challenge isn't whether to do it — it's how to get it moving."
**THE PIVOT:** Don't challenge the stall. Reframe as: "What if we could de-risk the restart? A small proof-of-value that doesn't require full commitment."
**THE EVIDENCE:** Reference the time already invested and the cost of continued delay (G-02 Cost of Inaction).
