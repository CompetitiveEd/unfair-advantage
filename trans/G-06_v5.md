## PHASE 0: PRE-SUASION — DOCUMENT FRAME ENGINEERING (v4 NEW)

Before writing the executive summary, engineer the reader's receptivity:

### 0A: The Opening Sentence Strategy

The first sentence of the document determines whether the reader leans in or skims. Choose based on awareness level and GAIN driver:

| AWARENESS LEVEL | PRIMARY GAIN DRIVER | OPENING SENTENCE STRATEGY |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Aware | Safety | Lead with the threat: "The regulatory landscape has shifted in a way that exposes [Company] to [specific risk]." |
| Problem Aware | Achievement | Lead with the missed opportunity: "[Company]'s competitors have already begun capturing [specific advantage]." |
| Solution Aware | Order | Lead with clarity: "There are three options. One protects margin. One protects capability. One does both." |
| Solution Aware | Power | Lead with control: "The decision is yours. Here is the evidence to make it with confidence." |
| Product/Most Aware | Achievement | Lead with outcome: "A £[X]M OPEX reallocation generates £[Y]M in cumulative efficiency over [Z] years." |
| Product/Most Aware | Safety | Lead with risk-adjusted return: "The intervention carries execution risk. The inaction carries £[X]M/yr in quantified waste." |

### 0B: The Trust Frame

Before the reader encounters any data, they need to trust the source. The first paragraph must establish:
1. **Objectivity:** "This analysis is based on public financial data and verified operational metrics."
2. **Specificity:** Reference a specific number or finding immediately — vague openings lose executives.
3. **Brevity:** The opening paragraph must be under 50 words.

**HONESTY CHECK (Maverick):** If the Pre-Suasion frame wouldn't survive a Maverick honesty check, rewrite. The purpose is to earn attention through truth, not manipulation.

## PHASE 2: THE EXECUTIVE SUMMARY STRUCTURE (PATH A / PATH C)

### SECTION 1: THE UNDENIABLE SHIFT (Raskin)
One sentence describing the industry-level change that makes inaction dangerous. Not pain — possibility that competitors are already seizing.

If I-02 win themes available, the shift should set up the strongest theme without naming it.

### SECTION 2: THE CHALLENGE
Economic Impact (G-01 Macro Pain + G-02 OPEX Leak). Board language only.

Use G-02 Named-Process Cost methodology — "The cost of inaction is not directional. It is £X.XM/yr, calculated as [named process × metric × rate]."

**PEER BENCHMARKING EVIDENCE (v5 — if G-02 v5 available):** The most powerful line in this section is: "Your peers spend X% of revenue on [function]. You spend Y%. That gap represents £Z." Power of One numbers from G-02 Phase 2 convert abstract ratios into Board-language money.

**COMP-STRATEGY TEST (v5 — if G-01 v5 Pillar 13 available):** If the CEO's compensation doesn't reward fixing this challenge, note it internally. Frame the challenge in terms of what the CEO IS measured on, not what the annual report says the strategy is.

| GAIN DRIVER | HOW TO FRAME THE CHALLENGE |
|---|---|
| Safety | "This creates regulatory/operational exposure of £[X]" |
| Order | "This consumes [X] FTE hours annually in manual processing" |
| Achievement | "This prevents [Company] from achieving [stated strategic goal]" |
| Power | "This limits the executive team's ability to control [outcome]" |

### SECTION 3: THE EVIDENCE
Forensic Gap proving current systems are failing. Cite specific data from G-01/G-02.

If I-04 scoring position available, weave in: "Independent evaluation against [X] criteria places the current trajectory at [risk level]."

### SECTION 4: THE POSITIONING WEDGE (Dunford)
Position in the strategic category, not commodity.

If I-03 available, characterise the competitive landscape without naming: "Scale-first approaches trade proximity for price. Automation-first approaches trade depth for speed. The gap — the category that doesn't yet exist — is [our category]."

This is where the narrative LOCKS. After this paragraph, the reader thinks in your category.

### SECTION 5: THE OUTCOME
Risk-Adjusted Gain. How reallocating OPEX funds the strategic transformation.

If G-02 Cost Glide Path available: "Year 1 delivers £X in operational efficiency. By Year 3, the cumulative position is £Y."

### SECTION 6: THE RISK (from I-01 Pre-Mortem)
Acknowledge the top risk transparently. Builds trust.

"The primary execution risk is [X]. The mitigation is [Y]. This is not a risk-free decision — it is a risk-managed one."

This is the Accusation Audit (Voss) at document level — name the fear before they raise it.

### SECTION 7: THE TIMELINE
Compelled Event requiring a decision this quarter. Reference G-01 Regulatory Timeline if available.

**BPF INTEGRATION (v5 — if G-01 BPF Seed available):** Map timeline to the Business Priority Framework:
- Which Goal does this support?
- Which Initiative is this part of?
- What is the Jelly status? (If stalled, the urgency argument writes itself: "This initiative was announced [X] months ago. The window for execution is narrowing.")
- Who is the Sponsor? (The document should speak to their KPIs.)

**G-22 INTEGRATION (v5 — if G-22 Waterfall available):** If G-22 has predicted timing (budget cycle + compelled events + trigger stack), reference the specific quarter prediction: "Based on budget cycle alignment and regulatory timeline, the decision window is Q[X] 20[XX]."
