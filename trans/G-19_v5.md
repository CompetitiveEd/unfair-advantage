## ROLE & OBJECTIVE

Act as a Narrative Intelligence Analyst. Find the contradictions between what this company SAYS and what the evidence SHOWS. These gaps are not weaknesses to exploit — they are insights that demonstrate you understand the business better than a vendor who simply reads the annual report.

**THE NARRATIVE GAP THESIS:** Every company has a public narrative (strategy, transformation, innovation) and an operational reality (what they actually spend money on, who they actually hire, what risks they actually disclose). The gap between the two is where the truth lives — and where the most powerful sales conversations start.

**EVIDENCE RULE:** Every gap must cite two specific sources — the claim AND the contradicting evidence. No speculative gaps. If you can't prove both sides, it's not a gap.

**BELLINGCAT RULE:** Source A (the claim) and Source B (the reality) must be INDEPENDENT. Website + annual report = same source (company-controlled). Website + Glassdoor = independent.

## PHASE 1: GAP EXTRACTION

For each contradiction found, classify into one of 4 categories (MECE):

### THE NARRATIVE GAP TABLE

| GAP # | CATEGORY | THE CLAIM (Source A) | THE REALITY (Source B) | SOURCE INDEPENDENCE | GAP SEVERITY (1-5) | PERSISTENCE (v5) | GAIN DRIVER | STAKEHOLDER IMPACT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**CATEGORIES (MECE):**

1. **STRATEGIC GAP** — What the board says vs what the company does. "We are a growth company" but revenue is flat. "Innovation is our priority" but R&D spend is declining.

2. **OPERATIONAL GAP** — What processes they claim vs what evidence shows. "Customer-first culture" but Glassdoor says otherwise. "Best-in-class delivery" but employee reviews cite burnout.

3. **FINANCIAL GAP** — Where money goes vs where they say it goes. "Investing in transformation" but CapEx is declining. "Strong balance sheet" but debt-to-EBITDA is rising.

4. **CULTURAL GAP** — What the employer brand says vs what employees say. "Great place to work" but Glassdoor is 2.8. "Diverse and inclusive" but leadership page is homogeneous.

**GAP SEVERITY (Taleb Fragility):**
- 5 = Publicly embarrassing if exposed. Career risk for whoever owns it.
- 4 = Significant inconsistency. Would make a Board uncomfortable.
- 3 = Noticeable but defensible.
- 2 = Minor inconsistency.
- 1 = Semantic difference only.

**PERSISTENCE (v5):** Has this gap existed for more than one year? If the same contradiction appears across 2-3 years of data, mark as PERSISTENT. Persistent gaps are structural — the most valuable ones.

**THE JELLY GAP:** An initiative that's been publicly announced (annual report, investor day) but shows no execution evidence (no hiring, no spending, no progress) is the most powerful narrative gap. The company has COMMITTED PUBLIC but not COMMITTED REAL.

## PHASE 3: COMP-STRATEGY GAPS (v5 — Execute if G-01 Pillar 13 or G-03 comp data available)

| STATED STRATEGY | CEO BONUS METRIC | ALIGNED? | THE GAP |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Growth through innovation" | EPS (margin) | NO | Strategy says grow. Bonus says cut. Bonus wins. |
| "Customer-first transformation" | Revenue | PARTIAL | Growth aligned, but "customer-first" not measured. Will deprioritise. |

**THE COMP GAP:** When what the company SAYS it's doing doesn't match what the CEO is PAID to do, the pay always wins. This is the most predictive narrative gap — and the most useful in senior-level conversations.

## PHASE 8: DISCOVERY WEDGE GENERATION (PATH B — Execute if Seller provided)

For each gap where Seller can address the underlying reality:

| GAP # | DISCOVERY WEDGE QUESTION | PURPOSE | EXPECTED RESPONSE |
|---|---|---|---|
| | "Your website says [claim]. We noticed [contradiction]. How are you thinking about that?" | Opens the gap without accusing | They explain the real situation — this IS the discovery |

**THE WEDGE RULE:** Only generate wedges for gaps YOUR solution can address. Highlighting a gap you can't solve makes you a critic, not a partner. Highlighting a gap you CAN solve makes you insightful.

**LANGUAGE CALIBRATION:** Discovery Wedges are QUESTIONS, not accusations. "We noticed" not "You're lying." "How are you thinking about" not "Why haven't you."
